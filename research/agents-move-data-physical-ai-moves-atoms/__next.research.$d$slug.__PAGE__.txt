1e:Tead,<p>A factory does two kinds of work. Some of it moves data: an enquiry is read, a past job is looked up, a quote is written, an approval is given. The rest moves atoms: steel is welded, a sack is lifted, a box comes off a truck.</p>
<p>AI can now help with both. But the two are not the same kind of risk, and a company that treats them the same will either move too slowly in the office or too fast on the floor.</p>
<h2>A wrong draft and a wrong weld</h2>
<p>When an AI agent drafts a proposal badly, a person reads it, sighs and fixes it. The cost is a few minutes.</p>
<p>When software moves a robot badly, the cost is a scrapped part, a stopped line, or a person standing in the wrong place. Nobody gets to fix that with a red pen.</p>
<p>So we sort every job by one question: <strong>if this goes wrong, what breaks?</strong> The answer puts the job on one of four rungs.</p>
<h2>The four rungs</h2>
<p><strong>1. The office.</strong> Agents read, match and draft. People approve. Nothing on the factory floor is touched. If the agent is wrong, a person catches it at the signature, because the agent never signs.</p>
<p><strong>2. Cameras that watch.</strong> A camera looks at the line and says what it sees: this part is flawed, this pallet is short. It does not move anything. If it is wrong, a person looks again.</p>
<p><strong>3. Software that moves the machine.</strong> Now the program decides where the torch goes, or which sack the arm lifts. A mistake has a physical cost, so this rung needs proof before it is allowed near real hardware.</p>
<p><strong>4. A body.</strong> A robot that works among people, at home or at work. This is where the whole field is heading, and it is the furthest away.</p>
<p>Each rung up asks for more trust than the one before. We think that trust has to be earned in order.</p>
<h2>Why the office is not a detour</h2>
<p>It is tempting for a robotics team to skip straight to the robot. We come from robotics, and we chose not to.</p>
<p>The office teaches you the company. Which jobs it has done before. What it calls its own operations. Who signs what. A robot cell designed without that knowledge is designed for a factory that does not exist.</p>
<p>The skills also carry over. An agent that reads an enquiry and finds the three closest past projects is doing the same thing, underneath, as a camera system that looks at a part and finds what it most resembles. In both cases the hard part is not the answer. It is knowing how sure you are, and saying so.</p>
<h2>What we hold ourselves to</h2>
<p>Four habits, on every rung:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>A person decides.</strong> Agents draft. People sign. On the floor, a person is in the room for the first run.</li>
<li><strong>Practice comes first.</strong> Every robot move is tried in a computer before a robot makes it.</li>
<li><strong>The maker&#39;s own check.</strong> A robot program is run in the robot maker&#39;s own simulation software, not only in ours, before it goes near the real arm.</li>
<li><strong>One honest line.</strong> Each piece of work is marked with what works today and what is still the plan.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Where we are today</h2>
<p>On the first rung, we are building agentic workflows with a first customer. Some of it works today, and some is still planned.</p>
<p>On the second, camera inspection is planned. We have not built a station yet.</p>
<p>On the third, our welding software writes a robot&#39;s program from a 3D model and tests it on screen. It has not yet welded a real part. A robot cell for emptying sacks is being designed in simulation.</p>
<p>The fourth rung is later.</p>
<p>That is a slower story than a video of a robot doing something impressive. We think the order matters more than the speed.</p>
